Introduction

This portfolio documents my learning journey in the Digital Service Design course. It reflects the concepts, tools, and design methods that I learned during the course and connects them with the practical experience of designing a Student Well-being App prototype.

I am currently studying Business Information Technology at Haaga-Helia University of Applied Sciences. My background includes experience in sales, marketing, and operations management, and I am interested in how digital services can improve everyday experiences and make complex processes feel simpler and more human.

Jakob Nielsen – UX Principles

Jakob Nielsen’s usability principles emphasize that digital interfaces should be simple, predictable, and easy to use. Key principles include visibility of system status, consistency, user control, and error prevention.

When designing the Student Well-being App prototype, these principles guided our decisions about navigation, layout, clarity, and visual hierarchy. I learned that good UX is not only about making something attractive, but about reducing friction and helping users feel confident while using a service.

Working as a Digital Designer

The presentation by digital designer Félix Meens provided valuable insight into how digital design works in real industry environments. Designers often work in multidisciplinary teams that include developers, product managers, and business stakeholders.

This perspective helped me understand that UX design requires collaboration, communication, and continuous iteration. It also showed me that design is not a separate visual task, but a practical part of problem-solving in digital product development.

Don Norman – Living with Complexity

Don Norman explains that complexity cannot be completely removed from digital systems. Instead, designers should help users manage complexity through clear structure, feedback, and intuitive interfaces.

This idea influenced the design of our Student Well-being App, where we focused on simple daily check-ins rather than complex health analytics. I learned that meaningful design is often about deciding what to leave out, not only what to add.

Map – Sketch – Decide

The design process used in the course followed stages such as mapping user journeys, sketching possible solutions, and deciding on the best concept. These methods helped our team move from understanding the problem to creating design solutions in a more structured way.

Mapping gave us a broader overview of the service, sketching supported creativity, and deciding helped us focus on the most feasible and user-centered ideas. This process was useful because it prevented us from jumping directly into interface design without understanding the actual problem first.

Customer Journey Mapping

Journey mapping helped visualize how students interact with the well-being application. Typical steps include opening the app, recording mood, receiving suggestions, and reviewing weekly summaries.

Mapping these interactions helped us identify user pain points and improve the design. It also made me realize that a digital service should be evaluated as a full experience, not only as individual screens.

UI Design Principles

  • Consistency across screens
  • Clear visual hierarchy
  • Readable typography
  • Accessible interface
  • Intuitive navigation

These principles help ensure that digital interfaces remain understandable and easy to use. During our project, we used rounded cards, soft colors, and clear buttons to make the interface feel calm and friendly for students.

Persona

24-year-old university student in Finland

Kha Han is balancing studies, part-time work, and personal life. She often feels tired and stressed due to irregular sleep, long screen time, and a lack of structured self-care. She needs a simple and non-overwhelming digital tool that supports her well-being in a realistic way.

Critical Reflection

One important learning from this course is that design decisions should not be based solely on visual preference. Instead, they should be supported by user research and usability principles. This became clear in our project when some ideas looked appealing visually but did not improve the user experience.

Another insight was the importance of balancing simplicity with functionality. While minimal interfaces are easier to use, removing too many features may reduce usefulness. Through this course, I learned that good design is about prioritizing meaningful value, not only reducing visual complexity.

Finally, I learned that digital design should also consider emotional experiences. For a well-being application, the interface should feel supportive and encouraging rather than clinical. This changed my perspective on UX from being mostly functional to being both functional and empathetic.

Conclusion

This course provided valuable insight into how digital services are designed and evaluated. Through the combination of theoretical concepts and practical project work, I developed a stronger understanding of user-centered design, UX principles, prototyping, and usability testing.

The knowledge gained in this course will support my future development in digital service design and technology. Most importantly, I now see digital design not only as interface creation, but as a way to create more meaningful, usable, and human-centered services.
