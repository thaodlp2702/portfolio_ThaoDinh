Introduction

This portfolio documents my learning journey in the Digital Service Design course. The purpose of this portfolio is to reflect on the concepts, tools, and design methods that I learned during the course and to connect them with the practical experience of designing a Student Well-being App prototype.

I am currently studying Business Information Technology at Haaga-Helia University of Applied Sciences. My background includes experience in sales, marketing, and operations management, and I am interested in how digital services can improve everyday experiences.

Jakob Nielsen – UX Principles

Jakob Nielsen’s usability principles emphasize that digital interfaces should be simple, predictable, and easy to use. Key principles include visibility of system status, consistency, user control, and error prevention.

When designing the Student Well-being App prototype, these principles guided our decisions about navigation, layout, and visual hierarchy.

Working as a Digital Designer

The presentation by digital designer Félix Meens provided valuable insight into how digital design works in real industry environments. Designers often work in multidisciplinary teams that include developers, product managers, and business stakeholders.

This perspective helped me understand that UX design requires collaboration, communication, and continuous iteration.

Don Norman – Living with Complexity

Don Norman explains that complexity cannot be completely removed from digital systems. Instead, designers should help users manage complexity through clear structure, feedback, and intuitive interfaces.

This idea influenced the design of our Student Well-being App, where we focused on simple daily check-ins rather than complex health analytics.

Map – Sketch – Decide

The design process used in the course followed stages such as mapping user journeys, sketching possible solutions, and deciding on the best concept. These methods helped our team move from understanding the problem to creating design solutions.

Customer Journey Mapping

Journey mapping helped visualize how students interact with the well-being application. Typical steps include opening the app, recording mood, receiving suggestions, and reviewing weekly summaries.

Mapping these interactions helped us identify user pain points and improve the design.

UI Design Principles

  • Consistency
  • Clear visual hierarchy
  • Readable typography
  • Accessible interface
  • Intuitive navigation

These principles help ensure that digital interfaces remain easy to use and understandable for users.

Student Well-being App Project

In the course project, our team designed a prototype for a mobile application that helps university students track their well-being.

The app focuses on:

  • Daily mood check-in
  • Sleep and activity tracking
  • Simple suggestions for improving well-being
  • Weekly summary insights

Tools Used

  • Figma – prototype design
  • Canva – visual presentation
  • User surveys – understanding user needs
  • Prototype testing – usability feedback

Critical Reflection

One important learning from this course is that design decisions should not be based solely on visual preference. Instead, they should be supported by user research and usability principles.

Another insight was the importance of balancing simplicity with functionality. While minimal interfaces are easier to use, removing too many features may limit the usefulness of a digital service.

Finally, I learned that digital design should also consider emotional experiences. For a well-being application, the interface should feel supportive and encouraging rather than clinical.

Conclusion

This course provided valuable insight into how digital services are designed and evaluated. Through the combination of theoretical concepts and practical project work, I developed a stronger understanding of user-centered design, UX principles, and usability testing.

The knowledge gained in this course will support my future development in digital service design and technology.
